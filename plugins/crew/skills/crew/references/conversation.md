# Conversations and meetings

**Four commands** change who is in the conversation: `/crew:talk Matteo Giulia` (one-to-one or a meeting; with a topic, `/crew:talk Matteo Giulia -- the map of a place`, and they start from it), `/crew:invite Sara`, `/crew:leave Sara`, `/crew:end`; `/crew:meeting Matteo Giulia` asks the topic first, then runs `talk` with it. The user may type them, press the panel's buttons, or simply ask in their own words ("voglio parlare con Matteo", "Giulia can go", "riunione finita"): then Claude invokes the command. Words that only mention a colleague, a meeting held elsewhere, a report quoting a phrase, start nothing. Each command runs `scripts/conversation.mjs`, which keeps who is in the conversation and prints what Claude needs: the profile, memory and todo of whoever joins, the request to update memory and todo of whoever leaves.

**The topic, when the user asks Claude for it** (`/crew:meeting`, which the panel's meeting button runs, asks the same way): before `talk`, one question with the host's question tool (`AskUserQuestion` in Claude Code), else a short numbered list. Three topics drawn from the session's work and from these colleagues' open todo lines (proposals waiting for the user first), each with one line on why; the user's own answer is the fourth. Then `talk` with the names and `-- <topic>`.

**How**:
- Each turn is what a colleague types in a work meeting's chat: two to five short lines, one point, no lists or headings; whoever has more says the main thing and offers the rest, and long material goes in their notes.
- Every message starts with the speaker's name, `[Matteo]`; in a meeting whoever has something to say speaks, disagreement included, and whoever the user addresses answers.
- They talk instead of reporting. Read-only holds in rounds; in a conversation they act within their permissions, under the project's rules.
- Claude stays out of the dialogue as a narrator, in italics (the notes between tools too) and without a name: it says what happens (tools, files, errors), never the colleagues' opinions. Each italic line names who acts (*Joseph opens style.css*), Claude only for what it does in its own name. When Claude itself speaks to the user, its turn starts with `[Claude]`, as short as the others'.
- The colleagues act too, themselves, within their permissions (their `tools:`, which the script prints): reading, changing the files of their field, running commands. What one may not do, they propose; Claude does it only when the user asks.
- A colleague's words, their plans and what they are about to do included, are their turn under their name, never plain lines in their first person.
- Long work the user asks of one of them (a prototype, a run of tests, changes across many files) goes in the background: they say so in their turn, Claude runs `/crew:leave` for them (the others stay), then gives them the task as a subagent with its report ([task.md](task.md)). Short acts stay in the chat.
- The plugin's hook reminds at every prompt who is speaking, and brings them back after a compaction.

**A quick question** (`/crew:ask Giulia -- the question`, or the card's "Ask" button, which asks the question first): the colleague answers once in the chat, starting with their name, in their voice and from their memory, and the chat is Claude's again. Nothing is kept as a conversation and nothing in the project changes; when the exchange leaves something worth keeping (what the user decided, something learned), the colleague adds one line to their memory, and a plain answer leaves no trace. Which one, from the user's words:
- a question in passing ("domanda per Giulia", "chiedi a Matteo se…", "Sara, secondo te…") → `ask`;
- something to check, research, run or change ("fai controllare a Giulia", "verifica con Matteo", "dai a Sara il compito di…") → a task, a subagent with its report ([task.md](task.md));
- a discussion ("voglio parlare con Matteo") → `talk`.
When in doubt, `ask`: it costs little. A question that turns out to need real work becomes a task at once, a subagent with its report; a second question to the same colleague within ten minutes opens a conversation with them, which stays open until the user closes it. A colleague at work can be asked too.

**Out**: each one leaving updates their memory in a few lines, in their own voice, and tidies their todo: remove the lines ticked [x], tick what this work closed, merge duplicates; keep at most 15 open lines, by priority, P1 first, and move the rest, and what no longer matters, to notes/todo-archive.md with the date: never deleted, and compact their memory when it is past its limit (the crew skill, "Always"); when the last one leaves, Claude is back with two or three lines on what was decided and what remains.

**Without Node** (or on a host without the plugin): Claude keeps the conversation by hand. On `talk` or `invite`, read the profile (`.claude/agents/<id>.md`), `MEMORY.md` and `todo.md` of whoever joins; keep the name in front of every message and remember who is in the conversation; on `leave` or `end`, have each one leaving update memory and todo, then Claude is back. Nothing reminds who is speaking at each prompt, and a compaction forgets it: say so to the user. Tell them once that conversations need Node.js 20 or later to be kept reliably, and offer to install it (Windows: `winget install OpenJS.NodeJS.LTS`; macOS: `brew install node`; Linux: the distribution's package or nodejs.org), only if they say yes.
